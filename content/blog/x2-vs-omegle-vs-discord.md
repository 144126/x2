These three get compared constantly and they're not actually solving the same problem. Here's an honest, specific breakdown of how they differ — including where the other two beat x2, because a comparison that only flatters one side isn't useful to anyone.

## How you actually meet people

**Omegle** (shut down November 2023, included here as the reference point everyone still searches for): pure random pairing. Click, talk to a stranger, click again for a new one. Zero shared context by design — that was both the appeal (total unpredictability) and the eventual downfall (nothing to filter out bad actors or bots).

**Discord**: you join an existing server, usually built around a specific game, show, or community, and talk with whoever's active there. You're not randomly paired with anyone — you're stepping into an existing space that already has its own culture and history.

**x2**: search for a room built around a specific interest and join a live conversation already in progress, or get randomly matched 1:1, weighted toward people who share your interests instead of pure chance. A middle point between Omegle's randomness and Discord's "find the right existing server" model.

## Effort to get started

**Discord** requires an account, and then the real work starts — finding the right server among millions of them, with no guarantee the one you land in is active or actually about what you wanted. This is Discord's weakest point for a stranger with no existing invites: discovery is genuinely hard.

**Omegle** required nothing — that simplicity was the whole product, and it's exactly what a lot of "omegle alternative" searches are still trying to recreate.

**x2** is built to keep that same near-zero friction: you can search rooms and start chatting without finishing a signup first. An account gets created for your device automatically the first time you actually chat, and you can turn it into a full login later if you want it to follow you across devices — but nothing blocks you from starting.

## What happens after the first conversation

**Omegle**: nothing. It's gone, and even while it existed, there was no persistence — every "next" was a clean slate, for better and worse.

**Discord**: you're a member of that server indefinitely, whether it stays active for you or not. Great for communities you actually stick with; a slow accumulation of dead server memberships if you're browsing a lot.

**x2**: you keep the room you joined, and you can leave any time without it lingering. Somewhere between the two — persistent enough that a good conversation doesn't just vanish, without the long-term commitment of permanent server membership.

## Group vs. 1:1

**Omegle** was mostly 1:1 (text or video). **Discord** is built around persistent group spaces (channels, voice rooms) as the default mode. **x2** supports both — 1:1 matching and group rooms — and treats interest-matching as the connective layer across either mode.

## Moderation

**Discord** moderation is per-server and wildly inconsistent — some servers are tightly run, plenty aren't moderated at all. **Omegle's** moderation problems are well documented and were part of why it shut down. **x2** applies platform-level reporting/blocking on top of interest-matched pairing, which structurally reduces (though never eliminates) the kind of low-context, low-accountability interactions that caused Omegle the most trouble.

## Honest bottom line

If you already know exactly which community you want to be part of long-term, Discord will serve you better once you find the right server — that's a real strength, not a knock. If what you actually want is the low-friction, meet-someone-new-right-now feeling Omegle used to offer, but with actual shared context instead of pure chance, that's the gap x2 is built to fill.
